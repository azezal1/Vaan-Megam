TDMA Schedule
Planner & Optimizer

A centralized Python engine that uses Distance-2 Graph Colouring to assign collision-free time slots to TDMA radio nodes — handling direct-link and hidden-terminal interference while maximising spatial reuse.

Distance-2 DSATUR Colouring Spatial Reuse Hidden Terminal Prevention NetworkX 500 m Radio Range
How it works
01
Build Topology
Construct a NetworkX graph. Every pair of nodes within 500 m gets a direct edge representing a possible radio link.
02
Conflict Graph G²
Compute the squared graph — two nodes conflict if they are 1-hop (direct link) or 2-hop (hidden terminal via shared neighbour).
03
DSATUR Colouring
Apply the DSATUR heuristic — iteratively assign the smallest valid slot, always picking the most-saturated node next.
04
Verify & Output
Compact slot indices, verify d=1 and d=2 conflict-freedom, then emit the Slot x Node boolean schedule matrix.

Conflict Avoidance & Bounds
Distance-1 Direct Collision d = 1
Direct RF neighbors (distance ≤ 500 m). Transmitting simultaneously causes receiver saturation. Handled by primary edge constraints.
Distance-2 Hidden Terminal d = 2
Two nodes out of mutual range sharing a relay neighbor. Transmissions collide at the relay. Handled by squared graph G².
Spatial Reuse Multiplier d ≥ 3
Nodes separated by 3 or more hops can safely transmit in the exact same slot, maximizing frequency/channel efficiency.
